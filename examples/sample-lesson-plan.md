# Lesson plan: Moles calculations (AQA GCSE Chemistry, Higher)
**Student:** Student A | **Length:** 60 min | **Date prepared:** 2026-10-13
**Weak point:** rearranging formulas. **Status:** DRAFT for owner review.
**Spec link (owner to confirm wording against the AQA 8462 spec):** quantitative chemistry (relative formula mass, moles, reacting masses, concentration in mol/dm3, gas volume at RTP).

## Aim
By the end, Student A can rearrange n = m / Mr and c = n / V without a memory triangle failing, and use them in a one-step then two-step reacting-mass question.

## Plan
| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-5 | **Starter (no formulas):** "A box holds 12 pencils and each box weighs 300 g. How many boxes in 1.5 kg?" Then name the pattern: amount = total / each. |
| 5-20 | **Explain + worked example.** Key idea: 1 mole = Mr in grams. Write n = m / Mr. Rearrange by "do the same to both sides" in two visible steps (not just a triangle): multiply both sides by Mr, then divide by n. Worked example (different from the practice): Mr of MgO = 24 + 16 = 40; mass of 0.25 mol = 0.25 x 40 = 10 g. |
| 20-45 | **Practice Q1 to Q3** (below), Student A does the rearrangement as a written line *before* substituting numbers. |
| 45-55 | **Quick check** (2 questions below). |
| 55-60 | **Next session plan** and homework (owner to set, not included here). |

## Analogy (reusable)
Moles are like "dozens": a mole is a fixed count of particles, and Mr tells you what one mole weighs in grams. Converting grams to moles is like converting kg of eggs to dozens.

## Common mistakes to watch for
- Dividing the wrong way round (m x Mr to find n).
- Using Ar of one atom instead of adding up Mr (forgetting the subscript, e.g. Cl in MgCl2 x2).
- Forgetting cm3 to dm3 (divide by 1000).
- Skipping the balanced-equation ratio in Q3.
- Rounding too early.

## Practice questions (original)
Use Ar: H 1, C 12, O 16, Na 23, Mg 24, Cl 35.5, Ca 40.

**Q1 (rearranging, 5 marks)**
a) Calculate the moles in 25 g of calcium carbonate, CaCO3. (2)
b) Calculate the mass of 0.30 mol of sodium hydroxide, NaOH. (2)
c) 8.0 g of a substance is 0.20 mol. Calculate its Mr. (1)

**Q2 (concentration, 4 marks)**
a) How many moles of HCl are in 250 cm3 of a 0.50 mol/dm3 solution? (2)
b) What volume, in cm3, of a 0.40 mol/dm3 solution contains 0.060 mol? (2)

**Q3 (reacting masses, 4 marks)**
Mg + 2HCl -> MgCl2 + H2
a) Calculate the moles of Mg in 2.4 g. (1)
b) Calculate the mass of MgCl2 formed from 2.4 g Mg (excess acid). (3)
*Extension:* what mass of Mg is needed to make 4.75 g of MgCl2?

**Quick check (3 marks)**
1) Moles in 11 g of CO2? (2)
2) Rearrange c = n / V to make V the subject. (1)

## Mark scheme
**Q1**
a) Mr = 40 + 12 + (3 x 16) = 100 (1); n = 25 / 100 = 0.25 mol (1)
b) Mr = 23 + 16 + 1 = 40 (1); m = 0.30 x 40 = 12 g (1)
c) Mr = m / n = 8.0 / 0.20 = 40 (1)

**Q2**
a) V = 250 / 1000 = 0.250 dm3 (1); n = 0.50 x 0.250 = 0.125 mol (1)
b) V = n / c = 0.060 / 0.40 = 0.15 dm3 (1); = 150 cm3 (1)

**Q3**
a) n = 2.4 / 24 = 0.10 mol (1)
b) 1:1 ratio Mg to MgCl2 so 0.10 mol MgCl2 (1); Mr = 24 + (2 x 35.5) = 95 (1); m = 0.10 x 95 = 9.5 g (1)
Extension: n = 4.75 / 95 = 0.050 mol; Mg = 0.050 x 24 = 1.2 g.

**Quick check**
1) Mr = 12 + 32 = 44 (1); n = 11 / 44 = 0.25 mol (1)
2) V = n / c (1)
